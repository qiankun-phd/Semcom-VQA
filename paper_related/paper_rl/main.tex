\begin{abstract}
Multi-unmanned-aerial-vehicle (UAV) visual question answering (VQA) in the
low-altitude economy couples a semantic-communication decision---which
\emph{level} of evidence (a cached answer, symbolic detector tokens, or the
image) to transmit for each question---with a safety-critical airspace
resource-allocation problem regulated by U-space separation management. We
make three contributions. First, we build, to our knowledge, the first
U-space/BUBBLES-conformant semantic evidence-scheduling simulation
framework in which every scenario parameter is traceable to a SESAR
Joint Undertaking deliverable; the separation kernel reproduces the
official G-4/G-5 numerics to within $0.18\%$. A corrected \emph{safety
bridge} couples the payload to the separation chain through an M/G/1
priority queue: token uploads leave the tactical-contact distance at the
BUBBLES baseline ($370.6$ vs.\ $370.5$\,m), while image uploads at peak
load inflate it by $23.7$\,m and forfeit $\approx\!6\%$ of relative
airspace capacity at $-5$\,dB---an asymmetric airspace tax. Second, we
formulate scheduling as a CMDP \emph{on the admitted set} with a
certificate-based escalation channel: a per-slot spec-attainability
certificate adjudicates unserveable safety-critical queries into a
budgeted escalation path ($\delta_{\mathrm{esc}}$ calibrated from the
certificate itself), and a risk-aware constrained hybrid-action PPO with
per-channel dual caps and warm-up is trained on top. Third, the same dual
machinery is operated across a feasible (nominal) and an infeasible
(peak-overload) condition and exhibits both textbook regimes: under
nominal the quality dual converges interior ($3.29$, never at its cap)
and the policy reaches $93\%$ of the escalation-aware oracle's admitted
mission success ($0.653$ vs.\ $0.703$); under peak the dual ratchets to
its cap on $3/3$ seeds---the classical infeasibility signature---the
constrained and unconstrained arms coincide, and safety is carried
structurally: the escalation-aware oracle attains $0.916$ only by
escalating $0.666$ of the critical load, while conflict, deadline-critical,
and escalation budgets hold ($0.003$ vs.\ $0.08$; $0.086$ vs.\ $0.155$).
A per-channel accounting table, a flat-PPO baseline, dual-machinery
ablations, four-axis zero-shot sweeps, and honest red lines (quality
channels are priced, approached, but not closed) complete a
reporting-standard evaluation.
\end{abstract}
